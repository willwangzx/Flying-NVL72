Beyond the atmosphere.

A preliminary satellite architecture for one complete NVIDIA GB200 NVL72: a pressurized compute capsule, deployable solar wings, pumped liquid radiators and optical downlinks. Sized to a provisional Starship cargo envelope.

Physics-based concept, not flight-qualified hardware.
The baseline closes the first-order energy, heat and mass budgets under the stated assumptions. It does not demonstrate that an unmodified commercial rack survives launch or radiation. Those are explicit engineering gates below.

“NVL72” here means GB200, 72 GPUs and 36 CPUs at a nominal 120 kW rack input. GB300 and later racks require a new vendor-specific design. No continuous internet service or untested hardware reliability is assumed.

Compute capsule

Keep the rack intact

Retain all compute and switch trays, copper NVLink backplane, management switches and original power shelves. Add launch restraint and close the airflow path through a liquid-cooled coil. The pressure hull maintains approximately terrestrial air density.

Energy and heat

Deploy the infrastructure

Four 5.5 × 50 m solar blankets and four 5 × 40 m radiator wings deploy on separate booms. Solar normals track the Sun; radiators present their emissive faces toward cold sky and keep direct solar incidence low.

Mission

600 km dawn–dusk SSO

Target inclination about 97.8°, 06:00/18:00 local-time plane; three-year design goal. Dawn–dusk reduces eclipse exposure, but the energy design uses a conservative 36-minute eclipse. Dedicated access windows and cached workloads support operation between contacts.

The first radiator factor 0.90 is an effective-area allowance for fin efficiency and obstruction; the second is infrared emissivity. The environment input is an absorbed heat flux on effective emitting area, not a temperature. Deep-space background is negligible here; Earth infrared, albedo, Sun and spacecraft mutual irradiation enter through the environment term. This is a uniform-temperature sizing model, not a nodal thermal analysis.

Charge/discharge efficiencies are each 95%. All cycle loss is conservatively assigned to the sunlit thermal peak. Excess array power is curtailed at the arrays, not dumped into the compute thermal loop. Array heating is rejected from the blankets themselves; their mutual heating contribution must stay inside the radiator environment allowance. The eclipse duration input is a stress-test setting and is not automatically derived from beta angle.

Only orbit period changes with altitude; radiation, drag, contact time, solar degradation and radiator geometry do not automatically change. The nominal calculator power excludes 5 kW electric propulsion; reduce rack power by at least 10 kW during burns. Fixed overhead is a sizing allocation, not a measured load curve.

Two independently isolatable 150 kW inverter trains feed the rack’s A/B power shelves. Their nominal 50–51 V DC outputs and bus bars stay inside the rack. Satellite power conversion is assumed 96% efficient; the rack’s own PSU losses are already included in its 120 kW input. [1] [3]

Design approximately 180 A per complete three-phase 415 V feed at 120 kW and 0.95 power factor. Allocate each redundant feed ≥220 A before detailed temperature and harness derating. Confirm shelf wiring, phase balance, grounding, transient current and PSU input requirements with the rack supplier.

Use isolated distribution, pre-charge, arc detection, current-limited branch protection and bonded chassis. High-voltage converters are sealed or vacuum qualified; launch inhibit prevents energizing them through low-pressure ascent conditions. An independent 28 V essential bus powers flight control even when the rack trips.

200 kWh battery: four isolated 50 kWh strings, 80% EOL capacity retention, 75% allowed depth of discharge. Delivers 114 kWh after discharge loss, versus 91.1 kWh for the design eclipse. About 1,667 kg at 120 Wh/kg installed pack-level density.

Maximum scheduled charging input: 120 kW. Nominal eclipse discharge is about 0.76 C on BOL nameplate. One string lost leaves 85.5 kWh deliverable: full-power operation through the worst eclipse no longer closes and must be curtailed. Battery life at repeated LEO cycling needs cell-level qualification; dawn–dusk is preferred to reduce cycles.

Array sizing and power failure behavior

Four independent wings, each 275 m², provide about 74.5 kW EOL each. The 1,100 m² calculation assumes 30% cell efficiency, 90% cell coverage, 80% combined EOL/operating-temperature factor, 97% tracking factor and 95% wiring/MPPT efficiency. These are design assumptions requiring vendor guarantees, especially cell temperature and degradation.

Baseline worst-eclipse requirement is 251.8 kW during sunlight, leaving 18.3% generation margin. Losing one solar wing reduces output to 223.5 kW; the maximum rack input with the same eclipse and 10% reserve falls to roughly 105 kW. Full 72-GPU hardware remains installed, but workload power must be capped. Safe mode isolates the rack and uses an allocated ≤8 kW essential load, with radiator flow bypass and freeze protection.

Thermal system: both liquid and air paths

Cold plates → heat exchangers → space

The commercial rack is hybrid cooled. For preliminary plumbing, assign 90% of its input heat to cold plates and 10% to capsule air; this split is an assumption to be measured. The allocation must be revised if shelf and networking heat make the air fraction larger. [1]

The selected 25°C inlet is an engineering target, not a verified GB200 coolant limit. Confirm chemistry, maximum temperature, differential pressure, required flow and CDU controls against the purchased rack’s interface document before procurement.

Two 100 kW primary heat-exchanger modules work in parallel, each with redundant pump heads and check valves. Use double-wall exchangers with monitored interspaces to prevent ammonia reaching the electronics compartment. The secondary system has four independent wing branches, each rated ≥60 kW, isolated by normally closed leak-control valves.

Why 800 m² of radiator?

Skin temperature: 294.15 K Emissivity: 0.90 Absorbed environment allowance: 80 W/m² Effective area factor: 0.90 Net capacity: 271.85 W per physical m² 800 m² → 217.5 kW Peak requirement with reserve: 161.6 kW Capacity / requirement − 1: 34.6%

Only one face of each panel is credited. Four wings provide 200 m² apiece; each comprises sixteen 5 × 2.5 m panels. Back faces use insulation and sun-rejection treatments. Thermally conductive facesheets and parallel fluid passages spread heat across each panel.

The environmental allowance is conditional: with solar absorptivity 0.10, direct sunlight alone can add 136 W/m² at normal incidence. A representative 80 W/m² allowance comprises 20 solar + 40 Earth IR + 20 albedo/mutual heating. Solar incidence must be nearly edge-on or shaded; radiator gimbals and orbit-dependent attitude scheduling enforce the envelope. [4] [7]

Critical bound: at 21°C and 800 m², the maximum absorbed environment compatible with 161.6 kW is about 158 W/m². At 180 W/m², full-load cooling fails. The hot-environment preset exposes this rather than hiding it in a “space is cold” assumption.

Flow sizing, pressure control and low-load operation
Primary: ṁ = 108 kW / (4.18 kJ/kg/K × 10 K) = 2.58 kg/s ≈ 155 L/min At 200 kPa Δp and 60% pump efficiency: P_pump ≈ 0.86 kW Air path, 12 kW and 15 K rise: ṁ_air ≈ 0.80 kg/s ≈ 0.68 m³/s at 1 bar External NH₃, 161.6 kW and 10 K rise: ṁ ≈ 3.44 kg/s using cp ≈ 4.7 kJ/kg/K ≈ 0.86 kg/s per wing

These are total flow estimates; branch balancing, cold-plate pressure drop, exact fluid properties and panel pressure loss remain to be measured. The 6 kW pumping/blower allocation accommodates pump losses, filters and ducting beyond the simple examples.

Microgravity provisions: fully flooded forced-circulation loops, bladder accumulators, capillary gas separators, degassed fill, bubble-tolerant startup and pump-inlet pressure monitoring. Do not depend on buoyancy, open reservoirs or gravity-fed CDUs.

Use pumped single-phase ammonia externally, following an established class of space thermal architecture, with a preliminary 2 MPa absolute operating pressure target to maintain subcooling near 28°C. Exact relief pressures and materials require pressure-system design. Place all ammonia outside the pressure hull and isolate it from rack coolant. [6]

At low load, mix warm bypass flow and progressively isolate panels. Heated accumulators and cold-end sensors prevent freezing; any isolated branch needs controlled drain-back into an accumulator or validated freeze/thaw tolerance. At 8 kW safe-mode load, do not continue flowing through all 800 m². Two minutes of thermal ride-through at full power would require about 18 MJ, so shutdown on loss of circulation is immediate; no large passive heat buffer is credited.

Failure containment

A single punctured panel is isolated at the panel-pair or wing manifold. Losing one entire 200 m² wing leaves about 163.1 kW at baseline conditions: barely above the reserved 161.6 kW peak, with insufficient practical uncertainty margin. Derate proactively. A 10% radiator-capacity reserve after that failure permits roughly a 110 kW rack input. Loss of two wings requires a much larger reduction.

Pump-speed, flow, pressure, coolant conductivity, moisture, temperature and leak sensors feed a separate safety controller. Flow loss first blocks new GPU work, then trips tray power if limits persist. A leaking cold-plate circuit can remove a tray from service; a complete 72-GPU domain after such a failure is not guaranteed.

05

Physical design and Starship accommodation

Pressure vessel and rack restraint

A 3.2 m internal-diameter vessel with a 4.4 m cylindrical barrel and two 0.8 m heads provides a 6.0 m overall core length. A 3.5 m external allocation includes insulation and debris shielding. A removable ground-integration head admits the complete rack. Normal operation uses approximately 100 kPa absolute dry air; this preserves conventional fan cooling and avoids treating vacuum as an acceptable rack environment.

Allow 1.2 × 1.5 × 2.5 m and 2,000 kg for the rack, plumbing manifolds and local restraint. These are reserved dimensions/mass, not OEM-certified specifications. Mount the rack to a load-carrying cradle with intermediate lateral supports, captive tray fasteners and restrained copper cable cartridges. Launch loads bypass ordinary rack casters and cabinet sheet metal.

First-order barrel hoop stress: σ_hoop ≈ p r / t = 100,000 × 1.6 / 0.005 = 32 MPa Illustrative shell thickness: 5 mm aluminium Provisional structural limit-load cases: 6 g axial and 2 g lateral, analysed separately 24.4 t × 6 g ≈ 1.44 MN axial interface load

The shell stress is a screening calculation only. Weld efficiency, openings, head stresses, fatigue, pressure proof/burst factors, buckling, acoustic vibration and launch coupled loads must be independently designed. 6 g / 2 g are concept assumptions, not published current Starship requirements.

Deployment sequence and deployed clearances
  1. Launch: rack off; coolant filled and secured; batteries isolated except essential bus; all wings and gimbals latched.
  2. Separate: verify launch-vehicle clearance, stabilize on the essential bus, acquire Sun and Earth, inspect pressure and fluid integrity.
  3. Radiators: articulated arms move the four packs clear of the upper deck one at a time; place two roots on each ±Y side at X = ±4 m, Y = ±8 m. Extend each 40 m panel chain outward, latch booms, then enable low-flow circulation. Initial booms hold the two parallel wings apart.
  4. Solar arrays: rotate the longitudinal cassettes and deploy two wings on each ±X side, with roots at X = ±5 m, Y = ±4 m. Each 50 m wing extends outward. In the illustrated deployed plan, solar blankets occupy |Y| ≤6.75 m, while radiator panels begin at |Y| =8 m, avoiding planform overlap. Radiator wings rotate about their long Y axes; the model shows their 5 m width vertical, edge-on to the illustrative +Z Sun direction. The thermal emitting face points outward along ±X.
  5. Commission: verify independent articulation and Sun/Earth keep-out angles, then energize 415 V feeds. Increase rack load in steps only after telemetry demonstrates stable coolant and air temperatures.
  6. Operate: slowly track array and radiator angles; fine-point communication terminals without slewing the full flexible structure. Never run the rack at full power with stowed radiators.

The static deployed span is approximately 110 × 96 m. This geometry is much larger than its launch package, but size alone does not prove structural stiffness, deployment reliability or thermal visibility. Fluid flex hoses at 15 hinges per radiator wing are a major life/pressure qualification item. Routing includes loop expansion accommodation, restrained bend radii and leak isolation; a validated hose design is still needed.

Mass, orbit control and lifetime

Mass allocation · baseline only

SubsystemkgBasis

20% growth is applied to dry mass, then propellants are added. These are parametric allocations, not supplier quotes. The mass budget does not automatically rescale with the calculator. The major uncertainty is the 800 m² radiator and deployment hardware.

Keep a large flexible platform controlled

Use a radiation-tolerant flight computer, dual star trackers, gyros and GNSS. Allocate four 250 N·m·s class control-moment gyros, magnetorquers for assistance and thruster unloading. This is actuator sizing intent, not a closed dynamics design; modal analysis and momentum accumulation determine final hardware.

At 4.56 μPa solar pressure, a 1,900 m² projected area could experience roughly 0.009 N before reflection effects. A one-metre pressure-center offset produces torque around 0.009 N·m, adding ~50 N·m·s per orbit. Aerodynamic torque can dominate at high density. Symmetric deployment and movable pointing geometry must keep the pressure center near the center of mass. Avoid rapid whole-body slews.

Propulsion allocation

A 5 kW electric thruster system at an assumed 1,600 s specific impulse uses 500 kg xenon, giving about 324 m/s ideal Δv at the allocated mass. A separate 230 s chemical system with 2,200 kg propellant gives about 217 m/s after electric-propellant consumption, reserved mainly for controlled disposal and urgent maneuvers.

Illustrative drag, not a density forecast: ρ = 1e−12 kg/m³; Cd = 2.2; A_projected = 800 m² v ≈ 7.56 km/s; m ≈ 24.4 t Δv_drag ≈ 0.5 ρ Cd A v² / m × 1 year ≈ 65 m/s/year; ~195 m/s over 3 years Tenfold density → tenfold drag demand.

Reserve electric Δv ~250 m/s for drag and ~40 m/s for orbit/collision management; remaining ~34 m/s is contingency. High solar activity or a larger effective drag area can exhaust this allocation. Dawn–dusk illumination does not eliminate atmospheric drag.

End of life: reserve ~175 m/s chemical Δv for a controlled reentry from 600 km, plus ~25 m/s navigation/operational allowance. A 600 × 50 km osculating orbit needs about 157 m/s impulsively at apogee; finite-burn, targeting and disposal margins still require trajectory analysis. Large surviving rack/pressure-vessel fragments preclude assuming harmless uncontrolled reentry.

07

Telecommunications and data flow

Bulk traffic

2 × 100 Gb/s optical

Two independently pointed 1550 nm terminals; one active, one redundant or used for handover. Baseline aggregate service is 100 Gb/s line rate, 80 Gb/s useful payload after coding/protocol allowance. Two simultaneous links are not counted in the service budget.

15 cm spacecraft apertures, 5 W transmitted optical power per active terminal, 1 m ground receiver and fine steering mirror. Coarse terminal pointing and fast tip/tilt isolation decouple link pointing from flexible-wing motion.

Fallback and command

Ka-band + S-band

Ka-band fallback target: 100 Mb/s through a gimballed 0.6 m spacecraft antenna, 20 W RF transmitter and 2.4 m ground dish. Use a separately powered, low-rate S-band transceiver with near-omnidirectional coverage for safe-mode command and telemetry.

Ground stations, spectrum coordination, modem compatibility and availability must be secured. No access to Starlink, relay constellations or third-party optical terminals is presumed.

Local autonomy

1 PB usable cache

Allocate about 1.3 PB raw flash for a 1 PB usable, redundant cache. A local dual-fabric 400 GbE gateway handles dataset staging, results and checkpoints; rack-internal NVLink remains untouched.

Signed jobs, authenticated control, encrypted transport, ECC, checksums, resumable transfers and reproducible checkpoints permit disconnected compute. Favor cached training, batch inference and orbital-data reduction over interactive internet service.

08

Radiation, debris and autonomous recovery

The largest uncertainty is the silicon

Neither pressure nor a thermal calculation makes GB200, HBM, flash or commercial PSUs radiation qualified. A high-inclination 600 km orbit encounters a different radiation environment from a terrestrial data center. Full-rack reliability cannot be inferred from a small computer flown on another mission.

Allocate 1,200 kg for local graded shielding around the compute volume, initially exploring approximately 10 mm aluminium plus 20 mm hydrogen-rich material over ~20 m², with remaining allowance for supports and penetration treatment. This is a mass/geometry hypothesis, not a dose guarantee; the pressure shell and debris layers add material but do not eliminate energetic particles or single-event effects.

Model the actual orbit, mission duration, geometry and solar environment, then test representative GPUs, CPU, HBM, NIC, SSD, power and BMC parts for TID, displacement damage where relevant, destructive latch-up and other single-event effects. Shielding can change secondary-particle production and must be optimized rather than blindly thickened. [9]

Fault containment without a resident technician

  • Independent radiation-tolerant controller owns pump safety, power isolation, attitude and communication recovery. A hung GPU job cannot disable spacecraft protection.
  • Current-limited tray branches and fast overcurrent trips reduce damage from latch-up where the hardware allows it. Supplier access is needed to implement protective interfaces.
  • Enable ECC and scrubbing where supported; log correctable errors, use application checksums and replicated checkpoints. ECC is not proof against all silent computation errors.
  • Checkpoint on radiation alerts or sustained error growth. Reset or isolate failed trays; expect reduced capacity and potential loss of the full 72-GPU domain.
  • Use spaced debris shielding on the pressure capsule, guarded hose routes and sectional radiator valves. Quantify debris impact risk across the entire 800 m² thermal surface.
  • Pressure loss, fire indicators, battery venting or major coolant leak cause rack shutdown. Batteries reside in thermally controlled, ventable compartments separate from the compute atmosphere.

First article

Start with the full rack, pressure enclosure and terrestrial coolant emulation. This demonstrates that the rack’s liquid and air heat can be collected before paying for the largest deployment systems.

What is not yet closed

Orbital thermal view factors, radiation lifetime, complete deployment kinematics, wing stiffness, battery ageing, contact availability, transient power, exact flight mass and present-day Starship interface.

Feasibility conclusion

No first-order energy or launch-volume contradiction appears in this architecture. It is a roughly 24 t orbital platform with ~100 m deployed spans, rather than a small satellite. Continuous full-rack availability remains unproven.
